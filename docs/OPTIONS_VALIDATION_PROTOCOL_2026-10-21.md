# Options PEAD strategy design and validation protocol

## Objective and scope

The decision is whether an option purchase following a *known* earnings surprise beats cash and the equity PEAD book after executable costs. Annualized return, Sharpe, and factor alpha are separate objectives; a rule may lead one while losing another. Predeclare a primary objective (net log growth at a fixed 20% outstanding-premium cap) and require Sharpe and factor alpha to clear their own gates. An abstain/no-trade option is part of the decision set.

The proposed research rule is a long call for a top-SUE-decile event or a long put for a bottom-decile event; middle deciles abstain. Direction is a hypothesis, not a guarantee: SUE predicts relative stock drift, while option P&L requires absolute movement that pays for spread, theta, and any implied-volatility change. A moderate OTM, low-pre-event-volatility call is a challenger because the corrected report's descriptive slice was positive. An approximately ATM call is the predeclared liquidity benchmark.

## Frozen candidate grid

For each event, compare absolute entry delta 0.30, 0.50, and 0.70; target expiration 75, 120, and 180 calendar days; and a hold of 20, 40, or 60 trading days. Use the closest actual expiration within 25 calendar days of the target, then closest delta within 0.10 of its target, with deterministic tie breaks. Require a positive entry bid, valid ask, ask at least $0.10, spread no more than 30% of ask, and at least 100 contracts of open interest. The planned exit must be at least 10 calendar days before expiration and must not cross an OptionMetrics coverage gap. A 10%-of-open-interest lot cap is an *illustrative capacity proxy*, not proof that the displayed ask can fill that size.

The grid code `pead/research/options_expiry_grid.py` reads underlying spots from raw `secprd` for all linked events, then streams raw yearly `opprcd` OptionMetrics Parquet files from the external drive twice: once for entry chains and once for selected option IDs at planned exits. It retains data in memory, then writes aggregate grid, annual, and same-event paired-comparison tables only. A missing or zero exit bid is valued at zero. The grid's premium-weighted ROI is a diagnostic; its cells do not represent a self-financing portfolio. With `--portfolio`, each cell instead receives its own sequential 20%-premium-cap, 0.25%-per-trade, 10%-of-open-interest book. The resulting CAGR uses realized settlements rather than daily option marks and has no valid marked Sharpe or alpha. Paired comparisons use the same event to distinguish a contract-choice difference from differing event coverage.

The faster `pead/research/options_robustness.py` test uses the *existing* Kelly-selected contracts (one expiry near 120 DTE) and compares 20/40/60-day holds. It sizes positions in entry-date order, with only prior events in the trailing-count estimate and without reusing same-close exit proceeds. Its end-of-trade P&L is recorded on settlement day; this is not a daily option mark, so its settled-NAV volatility and drawdown cannot stand in for mark-to-market Sharpe. The low-volatility cutoff is the first tercile of events from strictly earlier quarters after an eight-quarter warmup. It does not use the report's future-aware full-sample funded-position tercile.

## Required promotion gates

1. **Data and clock.** Verify announcement time versus purchase time, point-in-time SUE deciles, contract identifiers, splits/adjustments, and delistings. Any learned stock-return distribution may use a label only after that outcome has matured before the contract entry timestamp. Do not normalize order sizes over future announcements in the same quarter.
2. **Execution.** Buy at ask, sell at a positive bid, and treat a zero bid as zero proceeds. Resolve missing bids with corporate-action and alternate-date audits; report a zero-proceeds lower bound. Add commissions, spread stress, quote-size or open-interest and volume diagnostics, and realistic impact. Do not use an observed future exit bid to decide whether to enter.
3. **Portfolio accounting.** Size daily in timestamp order under a fixed outstanding-premium cap and per-name/option capacity. Fetch daily quotes for the selected final rule to mark live positions and compute true daily NAV, Sharpe, drawdown, beta, and factor alpha with HAC errors.
4. **Selection.** Fit choices in expanding past-only windows. Purge or embargo at least the maximum 60-trading-day holding period between training labels and each forward test. Choose among the *entire frozen grid* on the training data alone, including the no-trade baseline; append only untouched outer-fold returns to the final curve.
5. **Inference.** Use quarter or 60-day-block resampling for overlapping positions and a simultaneous multiple-testing adjustment across every tried rule. Report a 95% lower bound for net growth versus cash, plus Sharpe and alpha intervals, positive-year share, 2003/2013 leave-out checks, and capacity sensitivity. If any required gate fails, retain no-trade.

The 1998–2013 archive is already contaminated for confirmatory validation: the low-vol/OTM idea and historical trade years were chosen after inspecting it. A chronologically split result on this archive is useful for falsification and stability, but **cannot provide untouched proof**. Genuine forward validation needs later licensed option data or live paper trading with timestamped quotes. No finite backtest can establish a global future optimum.

## Compute and data handling

The scan is dominated by reading 17 compressed yearly `opprcd` files (about 29 GB) from the external HDD. The local machine can stream those files directly; a Google Cloud VM would not have access to the physically attached drive without transferring or remotely mounting licensed data. The current implementation does not copy those files. Derived inputs in the canonical checkout are read using `--data-dir`; research outputs stay in this worktree. Never delete this worktree or use `git worktree remove` on it: its ignored `data/` may later contain unique data.
