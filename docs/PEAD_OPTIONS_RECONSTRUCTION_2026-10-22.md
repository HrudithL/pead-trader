# PEAD options: reconstruction of the research line

## What the earlier work actually established

The underlying project began with analyst earnings-surprise events, prior-quarter SUE ranks, CRSP stock returns, and a post-announcement drift study. Its corrected equity implementations reported roughly 5.6% annual return / 1.24 Sharpe (`calm_weighted`) and 6.0% / 1.47 (`calm_rank_tilt`) in the historical file. Those are historical equity benchmarks with their own remaining sizing and full-sample-design limitations, not guaranteed investable returns. In particular, a bottom-SUE stock can underperform the market while still rising in absolute price; the stock long-short spread does not imply profitable purchased puts.

The option research then moved through five materially different stages. An even earlier `Options_Content` reference maximized raw expected return across strikes under a hand-entered three-state payoff table. That was a useful prototype, but neither the state probabilities nor unconstrained expected-value ranking established an investable edge; the project replaced it with empirical distributions and log-growth scoring before the issues below were discovered.

1. A decile-sorted **midpoint** study showed directional option-return differences. This is evidence of a signal in quoted mid values, not of ask-to-bid executable profit.
2. A near-120-calendar-day, near-50-delta contract rule and a Kelly contract selector used a learned distribution of future stock returns. The old 8.58% annual return / 3.78 Sharpe options result was later reduced to 0.282% / 0.156 with ask/bid treatment, and to −1.04% annual return when dead quotes were marked at zero. The prior engine also used sparse checkpoint marks, quarter-wide future candidate counts in sizing, and partial stale-quote carry.
3. The Kelly distribution was only **quarter-lagged**, not outcome-maturity-lagged. Its 60-trading-day labels from most immediately prior-quarter announcements could not have been known when a new quarter began. The audit found 179,874 of 180,633 eligible 60-day event outcomes (99.6%) mature on or after the following quarter begins. This invalidates the claim that the old contract selector was genuinely walk-forward for those training rows. The static `permno`–`secid` link and analyst estimate vintage also need independent point-in-time verification.
4. The historical GPU roadmap included a daily path builder and a stop/target optimizer, but **no real daily option paths exist** in the canonical data or this worktree. The stop/target optimizer's real-data branch raises `NotImplementedError`; its saved results are from 200 synthetic paths. The real checkpoint panel covers one Kelly/naive option per event at 0/1/5/10/20/40/60 sessions, not every contract or every day.
5. The fixed 54-cell grid uses **no Kelly distribution**. It examines top-SUE calls and bottom-SUE puts, 75/120/180 DTE targets, absolute delta .30/.50/.70, and 20/40/60-session exits. It buys at ask, marks and exits at bid, treats missing/zero exit bids as zero proceeds, and simulates a funded premium-cap book. The earlier archived result was settlement-only. This continuation corrected the CRSP/OptionMetrics calendar, corrected hold-specific sizing, applied full-source collision cleaning, and completed daily bid marking, daily French RF cash proxy, HAC factor alpha and joint year-block Sharpe tests for all 54 cells.

Three data construction corrections materially define the final sample. First, the shared calendar is the intersection of OptionMetrics and CRSP: 3,689 common sessions, eight OptionMetrics-only dates (including exchange holidays), and 336 CRSP-only dates. This intersection is applied before counting holding sessions and scheduling exits. Second, all cells now size using their own hold, not a default 60-session scaler. Third, event collisions are removed at source scope: from 147,142 event rows, all 27 rows in 13 repeated `(secid, day0_date)` clusters are excluded before decile selection. Fifteen of the removed rows were eligible extreme-SUE signals. Eleven clusters had multiple SUE deciles and one had both eligible call and put directions. The final eligible signal count is 65,512, versus 65,527 under the prior narrow filter. The final wealth leader is C/120/0.50/60 at 4.21% CAGR and 0.215 excess-RF Sharpe; C/120/0.30/60 has almost the same Sharpe and lower drawdown. The top Sharpe, C/75/0.30/60, funded only 246 contracts. Grid-wide Sharpe and alpha evidence remain inconclusive.

## Debate among the three independent reviews

The **mechanism reviewer** argued for a liquid top-SUE call benchmark: post-announcement stock drift can lift a call, but only if the saleable bid rise pays for both sides of the spread, theta, and any IV decline. Moderate OTM calls were a challenger because convex upside might be cheaper in that slice. The strict 120/50 cohort confirms a tail mechanism: on the same 6,023 selected contracts, average equal-premium ROI rises from −4.62% at 10 sessions to +5.67% at 60, but median ROI falls from −11.76% to −47.62%, while the profitable fraction falls from 38.7% to 35.9%. At portfolio level 60 sessions earns the most, but drawdown deepens to −33.2%. This is evidence that long holds capture rare large winners, not evidence that the typical contract improves.

The **pipeline auditor** found no real daily executable paths for the earlier optimizer, verified the Kelly training-label maturity flaw, and identified the information-clock and contract-deliverable gaps. The new aggregate daily paths solve the missing historical mark accounting, but not timestamped fill or displayed-depth uncertainty. For matched top-SUE calls at 120 DTE, the 0.50-delta rule's mean-ROI advantage over 0.70 delta ranges from −0.89 percentage points at a 20-session hold to +2.60 points at 60 sessions; its median paired advantage is negative at each tested hold (−6.45, −9.19, −9.09 points), and 0.50 delta wins only 30.6%, 28.6% and 26.1% of those event pairs. Mean return favors occasional convex winners; typical paired performance favors the deeper ITM call. The cell rankings therefore cannot be read as a universal strike preference.

The **skeptical reviewer** attacked portfolio inference. The earlier uncorrected settlement winner earned $4.179 million on $74.302 million of paid premium, with 68.9% of its profit coming from partial-year 2013; 2008–12 lost $2.370 million. Same-day `secid` priority could change funded trades under a binding cap, and the prior 10%-OI cap applied per entry instead of total open lots in an option. The corrected daily book fixes pro-rata same-day sizing and aggregate option OI. Its source-cleaned 120/50/60 result funds 6,023 trades, adds $4.865m over the rounded French RF proxy, yet has a −33.19% drawdown and negative uncertain alpha. The 54-cell Sharpe max-test p-value is 0.130; corrected settlement annual max-t p-value is 0.110. Nineteen individual four-factor HAC intervals are below zero and none is above zero, before correcting for 54 searched cells. The skeptical conclusion stands: historical positive dollars do not establish persistent PEAD alpha.

The synthesis is **a fixed historical call benchmark to audit**, not a verified production alpha strategy. Use C/120/0.50/60 when comparing to the historical wealth maximum; retain the 50-session variant as a less-drawdown alternative, not as a selected optimum. Do not promote the sparse C/75/0.30/60 Sharpe result. Calls are the only promising direction in the tested matching scheme, but calls-after-high-SUE versus puts-after-low-SUE does not isolate direction from signal side. A no-trade rule must remain eligible in every selection and deployment decision.

## Exact economics and identification problem

For one option share, the executable pre-financing gain is

`exit_bid − entry_ask = (exit_mid − entry_mid) − (entry_ask − entry_mid) − (exit_mid − exit_bid)`.

The first term contains underlying-price movement, time decay, IV/smile repricing, dividends, and corporate actions; the other two terms are the entry and exit half-spreads. A high-SUE call can therefore show positive *relative* PEAD and still lose money. Approximately .30 delta is out of the money, .50 near the money, and .70 in the money, but the grid sorts by distance to target DTE **before** distance to target delta. The funded contract's actual delta and strike/spot ratio must be reported rather than treating the cell label as its exact characteristics.

A purchased call's observed P&L alone does **not** identify PEAD alpha. Generic long market exposure, an event-stock premium, an IV risk premium, convex tail exposure, sector/size composition, and a particular crisis or rebound year can all mimic it. A credible PEAD test needs matched neutral-SUE calls of the same date, sector, size, delta, DTE, IV, and liquidity, plus market and nonlinear factor controls. A linear market intercept alone is an incomplete alpha measure for options. In the final funded leader, mean premium P&L is positive but 64.1% of trades lose money; the largest ten trades contribute $2.555m and there are 779 funded exits with a quoted zero bid plus 47 missing exit bids (zero proceeds). These facts make the distribution, quote quality and large-winner dependence central parts of the mechanism, not cosmetic diagnostics.

## Data and publication boundary

The attached external OptionMetrics files are streamed in place. The daily audit keeps selected contract quotes in memory and saves **only aggregate portfolio time series and diagnostics**. The French daily RF file is rounded to two decimals in percent and is zero on each day of 2003 and 2009–2013, so it is an imperfect cash benchmark. The retained worktree must never be archived or deleted because `data/` is ignored and may contain unique files. No Google Cloud VM is required for this local scan; a cloud VM cannot read the physical drive without a licensed transfer or remote mount.

The [original PEAD study](https://doi.org/10.2307/2491062) frames delayed stock-price response, while the [options-market earnings study](https://leiq.bus.umich.edu/papers/Lei_Wang_Yan_JBF_2020.pdf) motivates checking whether informed option trading has already reduced residual drift. [OptionMetrics describes IvyDB US as end-of-day option quote data](https://optionmetrics.com/data-products/), which explains why a same-close fill after an intraday announcement remains unverified. The [Options Industry Council explains how corporate actions can change deliverables and multipliers](https://prd-web.optionseducation.org/referencelibrary/optionsglossary); the raw quote files have `cfadj` and `ss_flag`, so the new audit excludes flagged contracts as a sensitivity. The public [Kenneth French daily factor library](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/Ken.french/data_library.html) supplies RF, market, size, value, and momentum for risk-adjusted diagnostics.
