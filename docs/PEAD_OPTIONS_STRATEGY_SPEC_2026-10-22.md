# Frozen PEAD call research strategy and promotion contract

## Decision and optimization objective

The fixed research benchmark is a **single long call** following a top-two-decile analyst SUE event: 120 calendar DTE target, 0.50 delta target, and 60-session exit. It had the largest funded CAGR/ending wealth point estimate of the 54 common-accounting rules. Actual funded contracts had median 122 DTE and 0.502 delta, corresponding to a median strike about 3.4% out of the money. C/120/0.30/60 is a drawdown-sensitive challenger with nearly the same Sharpe; C/75/0.30/60 had the highest observed Sharpe but only 246 funded contracts and 89 missing/zero exits. No cell is proven best or consistently profitable, and the intended *live* allocation remains zero. The comparison set includes the published French daily-RF cash proxy and the corrected equity PEAD sleeve; the rounded RF proxy is not an exact investable cash benchmark.

Annualized wealth growth, daily excess Sharpe, and risk-adjusted alpha are distinct targets. Increasing option premium spend can raise gross CAGR and lower Sharpe; a crisis put can have high average ROI but negative compounded wealth; a market-exposed call can earn positive dollars without PEAD alpha. There is no single mathematical optimum of all three objectives absent an explicit utility function, a capacity model, and a prospective distribution. The current primary historical diagnostic is **net incremental wealth over the all-cash French daily-RF proxy at a fixed 20% outstanding-premium cap**. Sharpe, factor alpha, loss concentration, and feasible fill size are separate required gates.

The benchmark produced 4.21% CAGR and 0.215 excess-RF Sharpe versus 2.34% for the French RF proxy, with −33.19% maximum drawdown and +$4.865m ending wealth over that proxy. Four-factor alpha was −1.78% annualized (HAC t=−0.89, 95% CI [−5.71%, +2.16%]). Across the 54 unadjusted HAC intervals, 19 are wholly negative and none wholly positive. The familywise bootstrap p-value for the best Sharpe across 54 cells was 0.130. These are in-sample candidate-selection results, not a live forecast. The French RF source rounds daily returns to two decimal places in percent and reports zero RF for every date in 2003 and 2009–2013; this likely understates bill carry in those years.

The only put cell above the French RF proxy is P/75/0.30/60, and its incremental ending wealth is only about $88k with 0.040 Sharpe and −89.7% median trade ROI. P/120/0.50/60 ends about $5.682m below the proxy. The grid's direction comparison is structurally confounded: calls are bought after top-decile events and puts after bottom-decile events. The data therefore support a *top-SUE call candidate* over the chosen low-SUE put construction, but cannot tell whether that gap comes from option direction, event direction, or both.

## Historical day-zero rule

1. **Signal:** analyst earnings surprise must be public before the contemplated trade. Rank SUE using only prior-quarter information and enter for decile 9 or 10. Abstain for other deciles. The upstream event table's estimate vintage and static security-ID mapping still require audit. This backtest uses an end-of-day `day0_date` quote, whose post-announcement executability is unverified; the next-session entry is the explicit feasibility sensitivity.
2. **Contract:** search calls with 120 ± 25 **calendar** days to expiration and absolute delta within 0.10 of 0.50. Choose smallest DTE distance, then delta distance, then spread, then option ID. This is the exact historical grid tie order; record actual DTE, delta, and strike/spot rather than assuming they equal their target labels. Require positive bid, ask ≥ $0.10, ask ≥ bid, spread ≤ 30% of ask, and OI ≥ 100. Strike dollars equal the OptionMetrics stored strike divided by 1,000. Reject a planned exit <10 calendar days before expiry.
3. **Hold:** sell on the 60th subsequent OptionMetrics trading date at its bid. Reject a schedule crossing a >10-day coverage gap. A missing or quoted-zero exit bid produces zero proceeds. An intraday stop/target is **not** specified: the old optimizer was run only on synthetic paths, and a stop based on daily close quotes would be another data-mined rule.
4. **Funding:** begin with $10 million. At each date, use the **prior close's marked NAV** to set a 20% cap on outstanding paid premium, a 0.25% per-signal budget, a 10%-of-observed-OI cap on *aggregate open lots in the same option ID*, and a 2%-of-NAV cap on aggregate open premium in one `secid`. The trailing-year signal-count scaler distributes the 20% budget over approximately 60-session holds. When a date's total desired premium exceeds capacity, scale every order by the same ratio and round lots down; no arbitrary security-ID priority determines the winner. Do not fund new entries using same-close exits.
5. **Daily wealth:** charge full ask and any added commission/impact at entry. Mark each live option at the day's bid. Add bid proceeds to cash when sold. If an intermediate quote is absent, report both zero-mark and last-observed-bid sensitivity; neither should be called an executable sale price. Apply the published French daily RF series to cash in the financing-aware run, and also report zero-yield cash for comparability. Label it an RF proxy: the daily file rounds percent returns to two decimals, making every RF entry zero in 2003 and 2009–2013. Include all 1998–2013 CRSP sessions in the denominator, including 2011 as a no-option-data year with no trades and RF cash. Schedule option entries and exits on the common CRSP/OptionMetrics session calendar. Save aggregate daily NAV only; keep selected option quotes in memory.
6. **Counterfactuals:** rerun at +1%, +3%, +5%, +7% of paid premium as extra all-in friction, at 1%/5%/10% of OI, without the per-name cap, after excluding any contract whose observed `cfadj != 1` or `ss_flag != 0`, and with next-session ask/bid/OI entry at the same predeclared exit. The delayed entry has 59 trading sessions remaining; it is a timing sensitivity, not a newly optimized 60-session rule.

For hold selection, the daily scan prices the **same 6,023 day-zero-selected 120/50 calls** at each scheduled exit from 10 through 60 sessions and reruns the funded account. Incremental wealth turns positive between 30 and 40 sessions. At trade level, average ROI improves from −4.62% at 10 sessions to +5.67% at 60, but median ROI declines from −11.76% to −47.62%; win share falls from 38.73% to 35.88%. Long duration helps the account through a small number of large winners, while the typical contract loses more premium. The 50- and 60-session excess Sharpe estimates are 0.214 and 0.215, with drawdowns of −28.70% and −33.19% respectively; 60 sessions has $0.409m more incremental wealth. This same-contract analysis reduces the changing-contract-selection problem. Because it reuses the inspected archive and its holding outcomes overlap, it does not validate an exact future hold rule.

## What a future selection procedure must do

The 54 historical cells share events and overlapping positions. A cell's high mean ROI can reflect tail events and varying event coverage rather than a better instrument on the *same* information. The corrected grid now has a matched daily funding book for all cells, but it still lacks matched neutral-SUE options and prospective selection. The event input had 147,142 source rows; the final cleaner removed all 27 rows in 13 repeated `(secid, day0_date)` clusters **before** filtering SUE deciles, reducing eligible signal rows by 15. A proper decision procedure should:

1. Freeze the exact candidate universe, order-sizing and event-time convention before observing test outcomes. Compare calls and puts, target DTE/delta, hold, pre-event volatility level and abnormal call-put IV spread interacted with option volume, and **abstain** with a common study window and a higher-precision cash benchmark. The IV spread feature must be measured strictly before the earnings release; the published options-literature evidence concerns announcement response and residual stock drift, not long-option returns.
2. On each outer test year or quarter, select only from earlier outcomes that had actually **matured** before its first decision date. Purge/embargo at least the maximum 60-session hold; do not use a prior-quarter event whose 60-day outcome extends into the test quarter. Rebuild SUE ranks, vol cutoffs, and distribution estimates within each as-of window.
3. Use exact same-event, equal-premium comparisons to identify the incremental effect of moneyness or tenor. Compare top-SUE calls with neutral-SUE same-date, size/sector/IV/liquidity-matched calls to identify PEAD-specific alpha. Add a beta- and convexity-aware stock/factor control, not merely a linear intercept.
4. Stitch untouched outer-fold funded daily NAVs; use quarter or 60-day-block resampling for overlapping positions and a simultaneous correction over *all* searched rules. Report lower confidence bounds, positive-year share, cash-relative wealth, max drawdown, and capacity/tick stress. A retrospective split of this already-inspected 1998–2013 archive is only a stability or falsification exercise. Later licensed data or timestamped prospective paper trading is required for confirmation.

## Data gates still open

- The end-of-day OptionMetrics quote has no intraday timestamp or displayed depth. Buying a historical best ask after a same-day announcement is an assumption, not a verified fill. The next-session test partially addresses information timing, not quote-size capacity.
- OptionMetrics `cfadj` and `ss_flag` identify possible adjustment or special-settlement complexity, but this extract has no explicit contract deliverable/multiplier field. Excluding flags is a robustness bound, not a full corporate-action reconciliation.
- The `secid` mapping is static; verify validity dates, earnings release times, estimate `statpers`, split adjustments, delistings, and missing bids before a live rule is considered.
- The 2011 option file is absent/corrupt and 2013 stops early. A 1998–2013 regression with all 2011 market days and no trades cannot fix that historical gap. The 2013 partial-year contribution should be shown separately.
- The benchmark's +$4.865m is relative to the rounded French RF series, not a reconstructed bill portfolio. Since RF is zero in the file on every day in 2003 and 2009–2013, its all-cash NAV likely understates cash carry. Recompute the comparison against a higher-precision, point-in-time bill series before interpreting the exact excess-wealth amount.
- No finite historical sample can prove a globally best future option or simultaneous maximal CAGR, Sharpe, and alpha. A strong in-sample result must still clear the prospective gates above.
